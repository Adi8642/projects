🎯 Introduction & Problem Statement
A global earthquake-tsunami risk assessment analyzes the potential for large-scale tsunamis generated by submarine earthquakes. This involves identifying seismic activity and modeling tsunami wave behavior to determine potential impacts on coastal populations, infrastructure, and economies. Risk is highest in tectonically active regions, particularly the Pacific "Ring of Fire," the Indian Ocean, and the Mediterranean Sea.

Challenges
While analyzing the Earthquake and Tsunami dataset, several challenges and limitations were identified that can affect data interpretation and model accuracy:

Limited Time Scope: Data from only one year (2022) restricts long-term trend analysis.

Geographic Gaps: Missing tectonic and regional details hinder fault-zone mapping.

Incomplete Metadata: Lacks information on duration, energy release, and aftershocks.

Event Imbalance: Only ~25% of events link to tsunamis, affecting model accuracy.

Measurement Inconsistency: Variations in CDI, MMI, and significance metrics reduce reliability.

No Impact Data: Absence of socioeconomic factors limits disaster impact assessment.

Complex Depth–Magnitude Link: Nonlinear relationships require advanced geophysical modeling.

Total records: 782

📊 Key Columns
Feature Description

magnitude : Earthquake magnitude (Richter scale)

depth : Depth of earthquake (km)

latitude, longitude : Geographic coordinates

cdi, mmi : Intensity measures (Community & Modified Mercalli Index)

sig : Significance score (impact)

nst, gap, dmin: Seismographic parameters

Year, Month : Event date information

tsunami : Binary (1 = tsunami occurred, 0 = no tsunami)

Conclusion

Based on our analysis of the earthquake and tsunami data, we can draw the following key conclusions:
In [ ]:
1. Temporal Distribution:
The number of major earthquakes (magnitude ≥ 6.0) varies significantly by year
Peak activity was observed in 2011-2012 with around 53 events per year
Monthly distribution shows higher activity in April and August (around 80 events each)
Relatively lower activity in June (42 events)

2. Magnitude Distribution:
The majority of earthquakes (417 events) fall in the 6.5-7.0 magnitude range
There is a clear exponential decrease in frequency as magnitude increases
Very few earthquakes (2 events) reached magnitude 9.0 or higher
Only 18 events were recorded in the 8.0-8.5 magnitude range

3. Depth Distribution:
Most earthquakes (589 events) occur at shallow depths (0-50 km)
There is a significant decrease in frequency with increasing depth
Only 7 events were recorded at depths of 400-500 km
A small cluster of 41 very deep earthquakes (500+ km) was observed

4. Tsunami Relationship:
Only a fraction of major earthquakes generate tsunamis
Tsunami-generating earthquakes tend to have higher magnitudes
Depth appears to be a factor in tsunami generation
Shallow earthquakes are more likely to generate tsunamis

5. Geographical Distribution:
Earthquakes are not evenly distributed globally
Clear concentration along tectonic plate boundaries
Pacific Ring of Fire shows significant activity
Both shallow and deep earthquakes follow distinct geographical patterns
